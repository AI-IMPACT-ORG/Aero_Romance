# Ankomst

The guest room was exactly as Katrine remembered it - her grandmother's study, with its worn wooden desk and shelves of medical journals. But now it held modern equipment too, a laptop and digital microscope standing alongside the traditional tools. The contrast between old and new was striking, yet somehow harmonious.

Mads had left her to settle in, but his presence lingered in the room. A fresh pot of sea buckthorn tea steamed on the desk, and the bed was made with crisp white linens. The window was open, letting in the Baltic wind and the scent of the herb garden below.

Katrine unpacked her medical bag, setting her grandmother's stethoscope beside the modern one. The old instrument seemed to hum with energy, as if responding to the island's unique atmosphere. She picked it up, running her fingers over the worn wood, remembering her grandmother's gentle hands.

The journal lay open on the desk, its pages filled with her grandmother's careful observations. Katrine sat down, her medical training warring with the island's wisdom. The symptoms of the Baltic Fever were consistent with an adaptive immune response, but the timing - always with the spring winds - suggested something more complex.

A knock at the door interrupted her thoughts. "Kom ind," she called. Come in.

Mads entered, carrying a tray of food. "I thought you might be hungry," he said. "The local bakery makes the best rugbrød." The dark rye bread was still warm, its rich, earthy scent filling the room.

"Tak," Katrine said, suddenly aware of how long it had been since she'd eaten. "You didn't have to."

Mads set the tray on the desk, his movements careful and precise. "It's part of the healing process," he said. "Your grandmother always said that good medicine starts with good food."

They ate in comfortable silence, the only sound the wind rustling through the herb garden. Katrine found herself watching Mads's hands as he spread butter on the bread - strong, capable hands that had delivered babies and prepared remedies with equal care.

"I've been studying your grandmother's notes," he said finally. "The Baltic Fever cases follow a pattern. They're more severe when the spring winds are strong, and the traditional remedies work better during storms."

Katrine nodded, her medical mind making connections. "The barometric pressure affects the body's adaptive response," she said. "And the plants..."

"Absorb the storm's energy," Mads finished. "Your grandmother believed they were part of the island's natural balance."

The wind picked up, rattling the window. Katrine stood, drawn to the garden below. The herbs swayed in the breeze, their movements almost hypnotic. "I need to see the patients," she said. "To understand the pattern."

Mads joined her at the window. "I've arranged for you to meet with the current cases tomorrow," he said. "But first, there's something you should see."

He led her to a small greenhouse at the edge of the garden. Inside, rows of plants glowed with an eerie light. "Storm's breath," he said. "It only blooms during the spring winds."

Katrine stepped closer, her medical training forgotten in the face of such beauty. The plants seemed to pulse with life, their white flowers emitting a soft, ethereal glow. "It's responding to the electrical charge in the air," she said, her voice hushed with wonder.

"Or to the island's energy," Mads replied. "Your grandmother used to say that healing wasn't just about treating symptoms. It was about understanding the connection between the body and its environment."

Their eyes met in the ghostly light, and Katrine felt something shift between them. It wasn't just professional respect anymore. It was something deeper, something that had been growing since the moment he'd caught her grandmother's stethoscope.

The wind howled around the greenhouse, but inside, the air was still and charged with possibility. Mads reached out, his hand hovering near hers. "Welcome to the mystery, Dr. Christiansen," he said softly.

Katrine's heart raced, but her voice was steady. "I think," she said, "that the mystery is just beginning."

The storm's breath plants glowed brighter, as if in agreement. The mystery of the Baltic Fever, the connection to her grandmother's legacy, the growing bond with Mads - it all felt like pieces of a puzzle falling into place.

The spring winds might have brought the Baltic Fever, but they'd also brought something else. Something that felt like the beginning of a new chapter in her life.

And this time, she was ready to explore it.
