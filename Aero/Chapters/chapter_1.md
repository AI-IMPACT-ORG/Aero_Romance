# Ægteskab

The hospital corridor stretched before Dr. Katrine Christiansen like an endless white tunnel of fluorescent lights and sterile air. Her white coat, usually a badge of pride, felt like a leaden weight on her shoulders. The sound of her heels echoed against the linoleum floor, a metronome counting down to a decision she didn't want to make.

The Copenhagen morning had arrived with its usual efficiency - grey, overcast, and precisely on schedule. Katrine had always appreciated that about the city: its predictability, its order, its clinical precision. But today, even the familiar scent of antiseptic and coffee from the break room couldn't lift her spirits. The memory of her grandmother's herb garden on Ærø, with its wild, untamed beauty, seemed to call to her from across the Baltic Sea.

She paused outside the administrative office, her hand hovering over the door handle. The polished brass reflected her face back at her - the same face that had earned her the nickname "The Ice Queen" among her colleagues. Not that she minded. In medicine, emotional distance was often as important as technical skill. But sometimes, in the quiet moments between surgeries, she wondered if her grandmother would have approved of such detachment.

The door opened before she could knock. Dr. Marcus Nielsen stood there, his perfectly tailored suit and practiced smile a stark contrast to her rumpled scrubs. "Ah, Dr. Christiansen. Right on time, as always." His Danish was perfect, but lacked the musical lilt of the Ærø dialect she remembered from childhood summers.

Katrine stepped into the office, her medical bag slipping from her shoulder. Marcus caught it with the same practiced ease he used to catch everything else in his life. "Careful," he said, his voice smooth as the mahogany desk between them. "Wouldn't want to damage the family heirloom."

The stethoscope in her bag had belonged to her grandmother, Dr. Marie Nielsen. It was one of the few things Katrine had kept from her childhood visits to Ærø. The island's medical traditions had seemed quaint then, a charming contrast to the cutting-edge medicine she practiced in Copenhagen. Now, the weight of it against her hip felt like an anchor to a different kind of healing.

"Have a seat," Marcus said, gesturing to the chair opposite his desk. The city view outside the window offered a glimpse of the life she was expected to embrace - prestigious positions, research opportunities, and, if the rumors were true, a personal proposal that would tie her future to his.

The coffee he offered was as perfect as everything else about him - strong, black, and served in fine china. Katrine took a sip, the bitterness matching her mood. "Hvad er dette om?" she asked, slipping into Danish. "What's this about, Marcus?"

He smiled, the kind of smile that had charmed donors and board members alike. "The medical board has approved your promotion to Head of Research. It's a prestigious position, Katrine. One that would put you at the forefront of medical innovation." He leaned forward, his eyes intent. "Your work on adaptive immune responses in isolated communities has caught their attention."

"And?" she prompted, knowing there was more. The scent of his cologne, expensive and subtle, filled the space between them.

"And," he continued, reaching into his desk drawer, "I thought it might be time to make our relationship official." He placed a small velvet box on the desk between them. "We make a good team, professionally and personally."

Katrine stared at the box, her coffee suddenly tasting like ash. The ring inside would be perfect, just like everything else about this proposal. A perfect match for a perfect life in a perfect city. But something about it felt wrong. The sterile office, the clinical precision of the moment, the way Marcus had timed everything like a well-executed medical procedure. It was all too... Copenhagen.

Her grandmother's voice echoed in her memory, speaking of island medicine and healing traditions. "Sometimes, Katrine," she'd said, her weathered hands gently crushing herbs between her fingers, "the heart knows what the mind cannot understand. Lægekunst er ikke kun videnskab - det er også kunst." Medicine is not just science - it's also art.

The stethoscope in her bag seemed to grow heavier, a silent reminder of a different path, a different kind of healing. One that didn't involve polished mahogany desks and perfectly timed proposals.

"I need time to think," she said, standing abruptly. Her coffee cup clattered against the saucer, the sound harsh in the quiet office.

Marcus's smile faltered, just for a moment. "Of course. Take all the time you need. The position will be waiting." His voice was smooth, but she caught the slight tightening of his jaw.

Katrine nodded, her medical bag once again slipping from her shoulder as she turned to leave. This time, she let it fall. The stethoscope inside seemed to pulse with a quiet urgency, like a patient's heartbeat calling her home.

The hospital corridor stretched before her again, but now it felt different. The endless white tunnel had become a path to somewhere else, somewhere that smelled of sea salt and herbs instead of antiseptic and coffee. The memory of her grandmother's garden, with its wild herbs and healing plants, seemed to pull at her heart.

As she walked, her heels no longer echoed like a metronome. Instead, they tapped out a new rhythm, one that sounded suspiciously like the beginning of an escape. The Baltic wind, though she couldn't feel it here in the heart of Copenhagen, seemed to whisper her name, calling her back to Ærø, back to the place where medicine was more than just science - it was art, it was tradition, it was home.

And for the first time in her carefully ordered life, Katrine Christiansen found herself wanting to answer that call.
