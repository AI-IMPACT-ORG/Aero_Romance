# Chapter 13: The Farewell

The morning of Ellie's departure dawned clear and bright. The sun rose over Ærø like a promise. The harbor bustled with activity. The ferry waited at the dock. Its engines hummed like a heartbeat. Ellie stood on the shore. Her bags were packed. Her medical bag was slung over her shoulder. Its weight was familiar as an old friend.

The island had gathered to say goodbye. The fishermen lined the harbor. Their weathered faces were etched with emotion. The shopkeepers stood in their doorways. Their hands were raised in farewell. The children from the school had made cards. Their bright colors fluttered in the breeze like butterflies.

Dr. Jensen approached. His steps were slow but sure. His cane tapped against the cobblestones like a metronome. "You've done good work here," he said. His voice was gruff with emotion. "The island won't forget you."

Ellie nodded. Her throat was tight with unshed tears. "I won't forget the island," she said. Her voice was steady despite the emotion welling up inside her. "It's changed me in ways I never expected."

Lily stood with Tom. Her face was pale in the morning light. Her eyes were red from crying. She held a small package in her hands. Its wrapping was bright as summer flowers. "This is for you," she said. Her voice was small but clear. "To remember us by."

Ellie took the package. Her hands trembled like leaves in the wind. She unwrapped it carefully. Inside was a small painting. It showed the harbor at sunset. The colors were vibrant as life itself. The brushstrokes were bold and sure. It was signed in the corner—"Lily."

"I've been practicing," Lily said. Her voice was soft as the morning breeze. "Mom says I have her eye for color."

Ellie held the painting close. Its colors seemed to glow in the morning light. "It's beautiful," she said. Her voice broke on the word. "I'll treasure it always."

Tom stepped forward. His face was carefully composed. But his eyes betrayed his emotion. "The ferry's about to leave," he said. His voice was rough with feeling. "You should board."

Ellie nodded. She turned to face the crowd. Her heart was full to bursting. "Thank you," she said. Her voice carried over the harbor like a bell. "For everything. For making me part of your community. For showing me what it means to belong."

The crowd murmured in response. Their voices were a gentle wave of sound. The ferry's horn sounded. Its call was sharp as a knife. Ellie picked up her bags. She walked toward the gangplank. Her steps were sure despite the tears in her eyes.

Tom and Lily followed her to the dock. Their presence was a warmth at her back. "We'll visit," Tom said. His voice was firm as the island's bedrock. "When you're settled in Copenhagen."

Ellie turned to face them one last time. The morning sun caught in their hair. It turned them to gold. "I'd like that," she said. Her voice was soft but sure. "Very much."

The gangplank was raised. The ferry pulled away from the dock. Ellie stood at the railing. She watched the island recede into the distance. Its familiar shape grew smaller. Its colors blurred in the morning mist. But its presence remained strong in her heart.

The medical bag sat at her feet. Its worn leather told stories of generations past. Her grandmother's words echoed in her mind like church bells across the water—"Sometimes the biggest storms bring the greatest clarity."

The ferry moved through the water. Its wake spread behind it like a path of diamonds. The island disappeared into the horizon. But its memory remained bright as the morning sun. Whatever lay ahead in Copenhagen, whatever challenges she would face, she would carry Ærø with her always.

Ready to begin again.
