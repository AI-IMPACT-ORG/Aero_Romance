# Chapter 7: The Investigation
# Kapitel 7: Undersøgelsen

The morning mist clung to Ærøskøbing like a patient to a diagnosis - persistent and slightly damp. Dr. Katrine Christiansen stood in the town hall, her grandmother's medical bag at her feet, watching the medical board officials arrange their papers with the precision of surgeons preparing for an operation. The rain tapped against the windows like a nervous heartbeat.

"Dr. Christiansen," the board chairman began, his voice as dry as the Copenhagen winter she'd left behind. "Your methods have raised... concerns."

Katrine's fingers found the smooth wood of her grandmother's stethoscope in her pocket. The town hall, usually a place of community gatherings and celebrations, had transformed into a courtroom. The island's elders sat in the front row, their weathered faces unreadable. Marie's portrait watched from the wall, her eyes seeming to hold the wisdom of generations.

"Concerns," Katrine repeated, her voice steady. "About what, exactly?"

"About the integration of traditional remedies with modern medicine," another board member said, adjusting his glasses. "About the scientific validity of these... methods."

The rain intensified, drumming against the windows. Katrine felt the weight of the community's gaze, the pressure of her grandmother's legacy, and the quiet support of Mads, who stood near the door, his presence as solid as the island itself.

---

The research laboratory hummed with the sound of modern medicine - centrifuges spinning, computers processing, the quiet beep of monitoring equipment. Katrine spread her grandmother's research notes across the table, the old paper contrasting with the sterile white of the laboratory walls.

"These are Marie's original observations," she said, pointing to carefully drawn diagrams of herb structures. "And here," she pulled up a digital display, "are the modern chemical analyses confirming their medicinal properties."

The board members leaned forward, their skepticism giving way to scientific curiosity. Thunder rumbled in the distance, a counterpoint to the precise whir of laboratory equipment.

"Fascinating," one of them murmured, examining the data. "The correlation between traditional knowledge and modern science is... unexpected."

Katrine allowed herself a small smile. "My grandmother always said that science and tradition are two sides of the same coin. We just need to know how to read both."

The laboratory's air was thick with the scent of antiseptic and the subtle aroma of drying herbs. Through the window, the herb garden swayed in the storm, each plant a testament to generations of careful observation and practice. The contrast between the sterile laboratory and the wild garden was as striking as the difference between a Copenhagen hospital and the island clinic.

---

The clinic's waiting room had transformed into a gathering place, patients sharing their experiences with Katrine's methods. The storm outside had reached its peak, rain lashing against the windows, but inside, the warmth of community filled the space. The scent of fresh-baked pastries from the bakery next door mingled with the herbal remedies, creating a uniquely Ærøskøbing atmosphere.

"Dr. Marie's remedies saved my son," an elderly fisherman said, his voice rough with emotion. "And Dr. Katrine's modern medicine helped him recover faster than any of us expected."

One by one, the patients spoke, their testimonies creating a tapestry of healing that spanned generations. The herb garden visible through the window swayed in the storm, its resilience a mirror of the island's spirit. The medical board members, used to the clinical efficiency of Copenhagen's hospitals, seemed taken aback by the warmth and personal nature of the island's approach to healthcare.

Katrine listened, her heart full. This was why she'd come to Ærø - not just to practice medicine, but to be part of something larger than herself. The medical board members took notes, their clinical detachment slowly giving way to understanding. The contrast between their formal suits and the islanders' practical clothing was as stark as the difference between a textbook diagnosis and a holistic treatment plan.

As the last patient finished speaking, the storm began to break. Sunlight filtered through the clouds, casting a golden light over the herb garden. Katrine felt a shift in the air, a change as subtle as the first signs of spring after a long winter. The medical board's expressions had softened, their skepticism giving way to something that looked suspiciously like respect.

The investigation wasn't over, but something had changed. The board had seen not just her methods, but the heart behind them - the perfect blend of tradition and innovation that made island medicine unique. The combination of modern medical equipment and traditional remedies was as natural here as the mix of Danish and island dialects in the waiting room.

And in that moment, Katrine knew that whatever the board's final decision, she had already won the most important battle: the trust and support of the community she had come to love. The island's approach to healing, like its weather, followed its own rhythm - and that was exactly how it should be.
