# Chapter 1: The Proposal
# Kapitel 1: Frieriet

The Copenhagen rain was as persistent as a patient with a chronic condition - it kept coming back no matter how many times you thought you'd seen the last of it. Dr. Katrine Christiansen watched it streak down her apartment window, each drop a reminder of the day's events. The weather, like her career, seemed stuck in a pattern she couldn't quite break.

Her medical bag sat by the door, still smelling faintly of antiseptic and the morning's emergency room. The proposal from Marcus, her colleague and now-former boyfriend, lay on the coffee table like an unwanted diagnosis. It wasn't that she didn't care for him - she did, in the way one cares for a particularly challenging case - but the thought of spending the rest of her life in Copenhagen's medical district made her feel like a patient waiting for a cure that would never come.

The apartment, with its modern Danish design and view of the hospital district, had been her home for the past five years. It was efficient, practical, and as sterile as a surgical suite. Perfect for a doctor on the rise. Or at least, it had been.

Katrine picked up her grandmother's necklace, the small Ærø charm catching the dim light. The silver felt warm against her fingers, a contrast to the cold efficiency of her current life. Her grandmother's voice echoed in her memory: "Sometimes, Katrine, the best medicine isn't found in a textbook."

A knock at the door interrupted her thoughts. Lily, her best friend and fellow doctor, stood in the hallway, her expression as serious as a medical board review. "I heard about Marcus," she said, stepping inside. "The entire hospital is talking about it. Apparently, he's taking the rejection about as well as a patient who's been told they can't have their favorite medication."

Katrine managed a small smile. "It's not that I don't appreciate the gesture," she said, gesturing to the ring box on the table. "It's just... I feel like I'm treating the symptoms instead of the cause."

Lily nodded, her medical bag thumping against the couch as she sat down. "And what's the cause, Dr. Christiansen?"

The question hung in the air like a diagnosis waiting to be confirmed. Katrine walked to the window, watching the rain blur the city lights. "I don't know," she admitted. "But I know it's not here. Not anymore."

The next morning, the rain had cleared, leaving Copenhagen sparkling like a freshly sterilized surgical instrument. Katrine's resignation letter sat on her desk at the hospital, as precise and professional as a medical report. Marcus's reaction had been predictable - a mix of professional concern and personal hurt, like a patient who couldn't understand why their treatment wasn't working.

"Are you sure about this?" he asked, his voice carrying the same tone he used when discussing a difficult case. "Your career is here. Your future is here."

But as Katrine packed her grandmother's medical journals and the old stethoscope that had been passed down through generations, she felt a certainty she hadn't experienced since her first successful surgery. The island of Ærø, with its sea mists and traditional healing practices, called to her like a patient in need of a different kind of care.

The ferry ticket to Ærø lay on top of her medical bag, a one-way journey to a place where modern medicine met ancient wisdom. As she closed the door to her apartment for the last time, Katrine felt the weight of her decision lift like a successful treatment plan. The future was uncertain, but for the first time in years, it felt like a diagnosis she could work with.

The Baltic Sea stretched before her, its surface as calm as a patient's steady heartbeat. The island of Ærø emerged from the morning mist like a treatment plan taking shape. Katrine adjusted her grandmother's necklace, feeling the small charm warm against her skin. Whatever lay ahead, she was ready to face it with the same determination she brought to her medical practice.

After all, as her grandmother used to say, "The best doctors know that sometimes the cure isn't in the medicine, but in the place where the medicine is made."
